# 0000011 The editing keys, and a history with no duplicates

- Status: accepted, and SUPERCEDED by `UI-redesign.md`, which supersedes every prior UI architecture ADR
- Date: 2026-10-05
- Supersedes: nothing. It fills a gap 0000010 left, and 0000010 refers here

## Context

0000010 is the API document for the interface being built. It gives the screen,
the commands, the prompt and the pane rules. It says nothing about the keys
that edit a line, and that is a gap rather than an omission: a reader who
cannot move the caret, erase a word or reach what they typed an hour ago is
not able to use the interface at all, so those keys belong to the same document
as the commands they sit beside.

The tree has a line editor at `internal/tui/line.go` and a key reader at
`internal/tui/keys.go`. Both are built for the frame 0000001 settled and neither
is what the interface being designed uses, so what is recorded here is the
decision and not an observation about the code.

The decision that needed the most care was the history order, because the
obvious spelling of it is the one that loses a reader's last edit.

## Decision

**`Ctrl+A` moves the caret to the start of the line. `Ctrl+W` erases the word
before the caret. `Ctrl+U` erases from the caret to the start of the line.**

The three are the emacs spellings rather than the vi ones, and the reason is
that the reader named them in those letters. `Ctrl+A` is not `Ctrl+B`, which
is the prefix, and there is no `0` or `^` in the form given.

**Up and Down walk the input history.** They move one entry per press and stop
at each end rather than wrapping, since a history that wraps sends a reader
looking for their first question to their most recent one.

**A history entry that duplicates an earlier one is kept once, at the position
of its most recent occurrence, and the history is ordered most recent first.**
So pressing Up after typing `ls` three times over an hour shows `ls` once, at
the top, and the older `ls` is gone rather than three rows down.

**A line that was sent is remembered whatever it was.** A question, a command
and a pasted paragraph are all history, since a reader reaching back for what
they wrote did not have to know which of the three it was.

**A line that was queued rather than sent is history too,** and is remembered
at the moment it is sent rather than at the moment it is queued, so the history
holds what the model was actually asked.

**Walking the history does not send anything and does not clear the line.**
Up replaces the field with the entry and leaves the caret at its end, so a
reader who walks back three entries and then presses Down is editing the field
rather than rewinding a transcript.

**A history entry that equals what is already in the field is not re-fetched
by a second press.** Pressing Up twice on one entry does not go somewhere
else, since there is nowhere else that is not a different entry.

## Consequences

`Ctrl+A` and the prefix are different keys, and a reader who wants to type a
control character into a model prompt has to know which is which. That cost is
accepted rather than solved, since solving it means moving the prefix.

Deduplication is what makes the history usable at all for a repeated command,
and it costs the reader the ability to reach the same text twice from two
different moments. A reader who ran `ls`, changed a file and ran `ls` again has
one `ls` in the history, and it is the second.

Deduplication happens on the exact text of the line. Two lines that differ only
in trailing whitespace are two entries, and trimming them would be a guess
about what the reader meant.

A pasted paragraph in the history makes Up slower to walk past, and there is
no bound proposed here. How many entries are kept, and whether a very long one
is shortened, is open.

The history is per session rather than global. A pane restored from disk gets
the history that was saved with it, if it was saved at all, and whether it was
is open.

## What is open

- How many history entries are kept, and whether an entry longer than some
  length is shortened or skipped.
- Whether the history is saved with the session, and whether `/restore` brings
  it back.
- Whether an input line carrying only a command word is remembered at all,
  since a reader who types `/pane 1` may not want it offered back.

## Alternatives considered

History most recent last, walked with Up going back in time. Rejected. It puts
the reader's most recent line two rows from the top of a list, and the first
press after finishing a turn lands somewhere they did not expect.

History with duplicates kept in order, so three `ls` presses give three `ls`
rows. Rejected. A reader who ran the same command three times has one thing to
reach for, and three identical rows is a list that has to be read rather than
scanned.

Wrapping at the ends of the history. Rejected. Reaching the oldest entry is not
something a reader does by accident, and a press that moves is a press that
surprises.

`Ctrl+E` and `Ctrl+K` for the ends and the tail. Not added. Nothing in the
design needs them yet, and a key added before a reader has asked for it is a
key someone has to read about.

## Notes

Nothing in the tree has changed. The editor in `internal/tui` implements its
own keys for a frame that is being replaced, and those keys were not consulted
in taking this decision.
