# 0000014 A restored session carries the whole conversation

- Status: accepted, and SUPERCEDED by `UI-redesign.md`, which Notion's record lists as superseding this record in full
- Date: 2026-10-05
- Supersedes: nothing. It answers a question 0000010 left open

## Context

0000010 settled `/restore` and said one thing about it that is not a decision:
context has to survive a restart for a restore to be worth anything, since a
session restored with its transcript but not its context is a transcript the
reader has to read rather than a conversation they resume.

It left open what is carried: the whole conversation, a window of it, a
compaction summary plus the recent turns, or the settings without the
transcript at all.

`internal/saved` writes one SQLite file per save with schema version 2, which
added one nullable column for the turns a tool call makes. The messages are
rows and an ordinary turn carries no envelope, which is what leaves room for a
field added later without migrating the files already written.

## Decision

**A restore carries the whole conversation.** Every turn, in order, as it was
recorded.

**It also carries the model, the approval mode and the pane permissions.** A
reader who restored a conversation and was then answered by a different model
would be reading a reply to a question they did not ask that model.

**The opening instructions are not part of what is restored.** They are read
again from the working directory, since a reader who moved the project has a
different set and restoring yesterday's copy would answer as yesterday's
program.

**A file written before a column existed is read, and a file written by a
later version is refused rather than half-read.** `internal/saved` already
does this, and the reason is recorded there: a conversation missing its tool
turns reads as though the model never called anything.

**A compaction summary is a recorded turn like any other** and is restored with
the rest, since it is what the model was actually carrying.

## Consequences

A restored conversation can be long, and the reader is the one who scrolls
it. That is the same thing they do with any long reply, so nothing new is
asked of them.

A restore can fail on a file a later version wrote. That is a refusal with a
reason rather than a partial read, and the reason is that a half-read
conversation is indistinguishable from an empty one.

The opening instructions being read again means a restore is not a pure file
operation: it depends on the directory being the one the reader is in. A
reader who restores while standing somewhere else gets that directory's
instructions with this conversation, and that is a thing worth naming rather
than a case to design around, since the alternative is answering as a program
that no longer exists.

The pane permissions are per-pane in the new design and a save is per-session,
so a restore brings back the permissions of the pane that was saved rather than
the set of panes. Which pane a restore lands in is not decided by this record.

## Alternatives considered

A window of the conversation, the recent turns. Rejected. A window is a
compaction that happens at restore time rather than during the session, and a
reader who restored and found the first twenty turns gone would have to decide
whether that was intended.

A compaction summary and no transcript. Rejected for the reason the reader gave
when they asked the question: it is a transcript the reader has to read.

The settings without the transcript. Rejected. A conversation with no words in
it is a configuration, and `/restore` is not that.

## What this leaves open

Which pane a restore lands in, and what happens to the panes that were open.

## Notes

Nothing in the tree has changed. `internal/saved` writes and reads whole
conversations already, and no `/restore` exists.
