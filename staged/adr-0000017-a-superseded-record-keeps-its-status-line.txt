# 0000017 A superseded record keeps its status line

- Status: accepted, and SUPERCEDED by `UI-redesign.md`, which supersedes every prior UI architecture ADR
- Date: 2026-10-05
- Supersedes: nothing. It settles how a record says it has been replaced

## Context

The records under `staged/` are numbered so a later one can supersede an
earlier one by name, and `adr-index.txt` carried the relation in a column and
in a section. The records themselves carried only `- Status: accepted` and
`- Supersedes: 0000001, in the part where...`, so a reader who had one superseded
file and not the index had to know to go and look for the replacement.

0000008 and 0000009 are both superseded by 0000010 and neither said so on its own
header. 0000001 and 0000002 are both superseded in the frame part, and 0000007 is
superseded in the arrangement while its session-per-pane part survives.

The word for the state existed in the index's prose and in nothing else.

## Decision

**Four words, in this order of strength: `accepted`, `proposed`,
`SUPERCEDED`, `rejected`.**

**`SUPERCEDED` goes on the status line of the record that was replaced**, in
the header, capitalised because it is a state and not a sentence, beside the
name of what replaced it:

```
- Status: accepted, and SUPERCEDED by 0000010
```

**`SUPERCEDES` is what the header of the replacing record already carries**,
under its own `Supersedes` line, naming the record and the part of it. The two
lines are read together, so a reader holding either file learns the other
without going to the index.

**A superseded record is read to find what was replaced, not to find what to
build.** The file is kept and is not edited. What it says is what was decided
at the time, and the thing worth keeping is the replaced alternative rather
than a version that agrees with the current code.

**A record that narrows, extends or corrects an earlier one without replacing
it is not a supersession.** 0000011 fills a gap 0000010 left, and 0000012 to 0000016 answer
questions 0000007 and 0000010 left open. All are `accepted`, and each names what it
extends.

**A record is superseded when its decision is replaced, not when the code it
described is later rebuilt.** 0000003 and 0000004 describe the connection probe and
the model choice, both of which are in the tree and unaffected by the
redesign, so both are `accepted` and neither is `SUPERCEDED`.

## Consequences

A reader who opens a superseded record and does not read past the header knows
before the prose that the prose is not what to build. The alternative is a
record that reads as current until its context is a third of the way down, and
a reader who stops early acts on a decision that was replaced.

The vocabulary has four words and one of them is unused. `rejected` has no
records, because a decision not taken belongs in `QUESTIONS.md` and then in
`ANSWERS.md`, and a record is written for what was decided rather than for what
was passed over. The word is in the vocabulary so that a future record has a
place to say it, and the fact that it has no members today is not a gap.

The index keeps its column and its section, and now duplicates what the
headers say. That is a duplication rather than a second source of truth: the
index is the view across all of them and the header is the one fact a reader
gets from a file alone.

## What this leaves open

Nothing.

## Alternatives considered

A `SUPERSEDED` marker in the filename rather than the header. Rejected. A
renamed file breaks every link to it, and the index, the design documents and
`SAVED.md` all refer to records by path.

Rewriting a superseded record to agree with its replacement. Rejected, and it
is the rule the index already states. A record that is edited to agree with
the code is no longer a record of what was decided.

Deleting a superseded record. Rejected for the same reason. The rejected
alternative is the thing worth keeping, and 0000005, 0000006, 0000008 and 0000009 all exist
because their replacements were taken.

The status as a fifth `Merged`-style enumerated field. Rejected. The `Merged`
field in the ledger is six values because the state is genuinely ambiguous and a
pair of booleans cannot hold it. A record is either current or replaced, and one
word says which.

## Notes

Nothing in the tree has changed. This is a convention over files that are not
committed, and the four records it changes are in `staged/`, which is ignored.
The status lines of 0000001, 0000002, 0000007, 0000008 and 0000009 are the ones that carry the
word, and they are edited to say so.
