# 0000032 /begin forks a pane, with a handoff note rather than full history, and copied permissions

- Status: accepted, and SUPERCEDED by `UI-redesign.md`, which Notion's record lists as superseding this record in full
- Date: 2026-10-05
- Supersedes: nothing. It introduces `/begin`, not asked by any numbered
  question in `QUESTIONS.md`

## Context

The reader wants a `/begin` command: take the current context and hand off
to a new pane to begin a task. Three things about it were settled directly
rather than derived: what happens to the pane that called it, what the new
pane inherits of the conversation, and what the new pane inherits of
permissions.

## Decision

**`/begin` forks; it does not hand off and leave.** The pane that calls
`/begin` stays open and keeps working on whatever it was doing. A second,
independent pane is created alongside it, not instead of it.

**The new pane's session starts with a handoff note, not the full
transcript.** It does not receive a copy of the calling pane's messages,
scroll position, queue, or copy buffer; it receives one note describing the
task, the way a colleague is handed a task rather than handed a transcript
of everything said before it was assigned. What exactly is in the note, and
who writes it, is not decided here.

**The new pane's permissions ledger is seeded with a copy of the calling
pane's current verdicts**, taken at the moment `/begin` runs. This is a copy,
not a link: a row is written for the new pane's id for each `(path,
verdict)` the calling pane currently holds, under 0000030's append-only rule,
and nothing further connects the two panes' ledgers after that moment.

## Reasoning

A fork rather than a handoff-and-close keeps `/begin` from being a special
case of `/close`: the calling pane's own task is not assumed finished just
because a new one started elsewhere, and nothing requires the reader to have
intended that.

A note rather than the full transcript matches how a human hands off a task:
the new pane is told what it needs to know, not shown everything that led to
knowing it. It also keeps the new pane's own session genuinely its own from
the start, consistent with every other per-session decision already taken:
the queue (0000012), the prompt (0000022), copy (0000025), and now this.

Copying permissions rather than starting at whatever a fresh pane would
default to is the one piece of context carried over deliberately: a task
handed off under an escalated directory should not have to re-earn that
escalation immediately, which a from-scratch default would force. Copying
rather than linking keeps each pane's ledger independently its own after the
fork, matching 0000030 and 0000031's per-pane rows rather than introducing shared
state between two panes for the first time in this design.

## Consequences

Escalating or revoking a permission in one of the two panes after the fork
has no effect on the other. They share a starting point, not an ongoing
connection.

A reader who forks a pane expecting the new one to know the whole
conversation so far will be surprised; only a note crosses, not the
transcript. What goes in that note is unresolved and is named below rather
than guessed at.

## Alternatives considered

Handing off and closing the calling pane, so `/begin` reads as a rename of
the task rather than a fork. Rejected by the reader directly: the calling
pane stays open and keeps working.

Copying the full transcript into the new pane. Rejected: it would make the
new pane's session start already carrying everything 0000012's reasoning holds
a session should own for itself, undermining the fresh-session shape every
other pane gets.

Linking the two panes' permissions rather than copying them, so an
escalation in one reaches the other. Not raised as an option; the reader's
answer was inheritance at the moment of the fork, which a copy satisfies and
a live link would overshoot.

## What this leaves open

What the handoff note contains and who writes it: the reader's own typed
task description, a model-generated summary of the conversation so far, or
both. Whether `/begin` takes an argument naming the task, or infers it from
context. Where the new pane's number comes from, under whichever pane-ID
rule question 25's open contradiction eventually settles.

## Notes

Nothing in the tree has changed. There is no `/begin` command and no pane
set in the tree.
