## Context

0000001 settled a program that owns the whole screen and draws every row of it
for one conversation. 0000007 replaced that with a multiplexer: a session per
pane, pane 0 as `tty0`, `Ctrl+B` as the prefix, and a global status line,
leaving the arrangement of the panes to be designed later.

The reader has now given that arrangement, and it is not the `tmux` one. The
screen is in three vertical parts rather than a tiling of panes:

1. the live view, at the top, which is where the model's output goes;
2. a status window beneath it, broken into multiline sections;
3. a pane view beneath that, carrying a pane number and pane activity.

The third part is a list rather than a rectangle. A reader scrolls the live
view, finds a numbered block, copies it, and pastes it back, so the parts of
the screen that are read at different moments are stacked rather than tiled.

Two other records are affected. `staged/status-interrupt.txt` is superseded by
0000006 and is named there. The frame that 0000001 settled is gone, and 0000001 is
superseded rather than corrected, since a record edited to agree with a
replacement is not a record.

## Decision

**The screen is three parts, top to bottom: live view, status window, pane
view.** Each is a region of the screen. The live view is the tallest and is
the one a reader reads.

**The live view is where the model writes.** It shows the question, the tool
calls, the answers, the reply, and the errors the model produced. A reader who
wants one of those is looking at this region and not at a pane they have to
switch to.

**The status window is broken into multiline sections.** A section is a named
block that occupies the rows it needs rather than one row of columns, so a
figure that needs three lines takes three lines and a figure that needs none
takes none. The candidates for sections are model, state, cost, the focused
pane, and the pane count, and the reader has not chosen the set.

**The pane view carries a pane number and pane activity.** Activity is a bell,
so a reader can see at a glance which panes have something new in them
without switching to each one. The pane view is how a reader finds the pane
they want.

**`/pane N` switches to pane N. `/pane next` and `/pane previous` step through
the panes**, wrapping at both ends, as `tmux` does. This is the pane
switching the reader asked to be reminded of, and it is written into the
records here so it is not rediscovered.

**A pane is still a session and pane 0 is still the main session.** That part
of 0000007 stands. What changes is that panes are listed rather than tiled, so a
pane is reached by naming it rather than by moving a boundary.

**`/paste` sends the reader's most recent copy back to the model.** The reader
scrolls the live view, finds a numbered fenced block, runs `/copy N M`, and
then runs `/paste` to put exactly that block in front of the model. The
selection is the whole point of the two commands together, so `/paste` pastes
what was last copied and nothing else. A reader who scrolls back and copies a
block has one thing to do next and it is this.

**`/paste` with nothing copied is refused by name.** A paste that silently
sends an empty message is a turn spent on nothing, and the reader cannot tell
it from a model that refused to answer.

## Consequences

The three parts compete for rows, so the layout has to say which part yields
when the terminal is short. That is a decision and it is open. The live view
should yield last, since a reader reading history is using it, and a status
window that cannot be read is a worse failure than a shorter live view.

The pane view is a list, so a reader with many panes cannot see them all at
once and the view has to scroll or fold. Which, is open.

`/pane` becomes the primary way to change panes and `Ctrl+B` is no longer the
only one. That is not a contradiction, since a prefix that steps panes and a
command that names one are two ways at the same thing, and the prefix is not
described here.

The pane view carries a bell, so something has to ring it. A bell that rings
on every arrival is a bell the reader stops hearing, and what rings it is
open.

## What is open

- What the status window sections are, in what order, and how many rows the
  window takes when the terminal is short.
- What rings the pane bell. Arrival of a reply, a reply needing an answer, or
  an error are three different things and they are not the same.
- Whether a pane view with more panes than rows scrolls, folds, or shows a
  count.

## Alternatives considered

Tiled panes, as `tmux` draws them. Rejected for the reason in the context. The
reader copies a block out of the live view and pastes it back, and that is one
region being read and one region being typed into, which stacking serves and
tiling does not.

One region with a status bar at the bottom, which is what the tree draws.
Rejected. The status window is multiline and has sections, and a bar cannot
carry a section.

A `/paste` that asks for what to paste. Rejected. The reader has just copied
it, and a prompt asking them to choose between one thing and nothing is a
prompt that exists to be dismissed.
