# 0000002 A status field is one character

- Status: accepted, and SUPERCEDED by 0000010. It stands as a record of a decision
  that was taken and then replaced
- Date: 2026-10-05
- Supersedes: bars of named fields, with whole fields dropped when the bar was too
  narrow, in an order by how much a reader loses by losing each

## Context

The previous frame drew bars and each bar carried several named fields. When a
bar did not fit the terminal, whole fields were dropped from the right, and the
order they were dropped in was chosen to minimize what a reader loses.

That was a reasonable rule, and it was a rule about losing information, which is
a sign the arrangement was wrong: the reader had to be told what they were not
being shown.

## Decision

A field is one character. The width is a floor and never a cap, so nothing is
dropped and nothing is cut, and a bar carries every field it has and overflows
its row rather than losing the end of itself.

Two fields are exceptions and are named as such rather than being special cases
in the drawing code. The state is carried as the whole word, since it is the field
a reader watches second by second and a state spelled out is a state where a
single letter is something a reader has to learn. The prompt row carries the
prompt and whatever the reader typed, since a row cannot be one character and a
prompt at once.

Every glyph is a letter a reader can read rather than a decorative figure.

## Consequences

The order-of-loss rule is gone, and with it the question of what a reader is not
being shown. Nothing is dropped at any width.

A count is one digit, since a field is one character. A counter in the tens of
thousands reports its first digit. The full figure is in the transcript and the
field names say which count each one is, so a reader wanting the figure can read
it from where it is written in full.

Twenty one-character fields occupy twenty columns of a screen and leave the rest
to the log beside them. A field that cannot say what it means in one character
cannot be drawn at all, which is a constraint on what may be added.

## Alternatives considered

Keep the bars at a readable width and let them be dropped field by field. This
was the previous arrangement and it was rejected for the reason in the context.

Let each field carry a short name rather than a glyph. A two-character field would
have carried more, at the cost of the column being two wide and of every field
needing an abbreviation that is its own vocabulary.

## Notes

The floor of one is what makes the overflow total rather than partial. A larger
floor would reintroduce a width at which something is dropped, which is the thing
this decision removes.

The rule this record rejected is the one that came back. 0000016 puts the shed order
back into the bars, with Verbosity falling off first, and a bar dropping whole at
one field rather than emptying gradually. That is a different decision with a
different reason, and 0000016 is the record of it. Read this for why a field that
cannot say what it means in one character cannot be drawn at all, and not for the
screen.
