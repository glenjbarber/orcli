# 0000015 The history is one hundred exact entries

- Status: accepted, and SUPERCEDED by `UI-redesign.md`, which supersedes every prior UI architecture ADR
- Date: 2026-10-05
- Supersedes: nothing. It answers a question 0000011 left open

## Context

0000011 settled the editing keys and the history, and left four things open
about the history: how many entries are kept, whether an entry longer than
some length is shortened or skipped, whether the history is saved with the
session, and whether a bare command word is remembered at all.

The reader answered the first and said the persistence half can be carried by
two commands that exist or are named for it: `/autosave` and `/autocompact`.
`/autosave` is in the command table in the tree and writes a session after a
clean turn and after an idle period. `/autocompact` is not in the tree.

## Decision

**One hundred entries.** The hundred most recent, and the oldest falls off.

**Entries are kept exact and nothing is shortened.** A pasted paragraph is a
history entry at its full length, because a reader reaching for what they
pasted wants what they pasted.

**Dedup is as 0000011 settled it.** A repeated entry is kept once at the position
of its most recent occurrence, the history is ordered most recent first, and
the comparison is on the exact text with no trimming.

**A bare command word is remembered like any other line.** A reader who types
`/pane 1` has it offered back, and a reader who does not want that uses
`Ctrl+U` or walks past it.

**The history is saved with the session and restored with it,** so a restored
session has the history the original had. What that costs, and when it is
written, is the work of `/autosave`.

**`/autocompact` bounds it.** It is the command that trims a long history, and
a reader who wants fewer entries asks for them rather than having the number
changed under them.

## Consequences

A hundred entries at full length is a real amount of text, and 0000011 said a
pasted paragraph makes Up slower to walk past. The bound is on count rather
than on length, so the cost is bounded by the reader pasting rather than by
the count.

Walking to the end of a hundred entries is a reader who has pressed Up a
hundred times, and that is not something to guard against.

The history is per session. A pane restored from disk gets the history that
was saved with it.

Keeping a bare command in the history means the history is a record of what
was typed rather than of what was asked, which is the decision 0000011 took and
this confirms rather than changes.

## What this leaves open

`/autocompact` is named here and does not exist. What it trims, what it
reports, and whether it can refuse are not decided, and nothing above depends
on the answer.

Whether the history is written on every autosave or on a slower cadence than
the conversation is the work of `/autosave` and is not decided here.

## Alternatives considered

Fifty entries, with an entry over four hundred columns kept whole anyway.
Rejected. The cap on length was the part worth having and it turned out to
mean nothing, since the rule it replaced already keeps entries whole. Fifty
entries is a bound with no reason behind it.

No bound at all. Rejected. A session left open for a week accumulates whatever
the reader typed, and the memory is unbounded for a list nobody reads past the
top of.

Keeping the history out of the save. Rejected. A reader who restores a
conversation and cannot reach what they asked last week has been given a
transcript rather than a conversation, which is the outcome 0000010 named as the
reason context has to survive.

## Notes

Nothing in the tree has changed. The line editor in `internal/tui` has its own
history behaviour for a frame that is being replaced, and neither
`/autosave` nor `/autocompact` is built.
