# 0000009 What the live view shows, and what is a joke

- Status: accepted, and SUPERCEDED by 0000010. It stands as a record of a decision
  that was taken and then replaced, and is read to find what was replaced rather
  than what to build, and SUPERCEDED by `UI-redesign.md`, which redefines the screen regions, status-bar fields, and prompt this record describes
- Date: 2026-10-05
- Supersedes: nothing. It records decisions 0000007 and 0000008 left open

## Context

0000008 settled the arrangement of the screen and left the live view's contents
unnamed. The reader has now said what goes in it, and three of those things
are decisions about what a reader sees rather than about where it sits.

The prompt is the fourth. It has been removed once already. The nine-row layout
that entries in the ledger record dropped `root@localhost $ ` because the
reader took it out, and it is coming back with the reader's own words attached
to it: as a joke.

## Decision

**The prompt is `root@localhost $ `.** It is a joke and it is drawn as one. It
is not derived from the reader's hostname, their user, or the directory, since
a prompt that computed itself would have to be honest and this one is not.

**Output errors are shown in the live view**, as rows beside the tool call or
reply they came from. An error a model produced is part of what the model
produced, and a reader who cannot see it will believe the tool worked.

**Input errors are shown in the input field and clear themselves.** They do not
become a row in the live view, since a row is the record of what happened and
an input error never became a question. They clear on a timeout rather than on
the next keystroke, so a reader who looked away sees the field recover.

**Tool output is toggle-able with `/verbose`, and it is off by default.** A
line such as `[fs] read_file [...]` is the shape of it. A reader who wants to
see what the model did reads it; a reader who wants the answer does not have
to scroll past it.

**A tool line carries an icon where a terminal can draw one.** A shell command
is a terminal icon. This is conditional in the design rather than in the record:
a figure the terminal cannot draw is a figure the program must fall back from,
and the fallback is a decision about the palette rather than about the row.

**The twiddle is a three by two block of single-dot Braille characters
carrying a new twiddle**: a horizontally scrolling swirl-in, swirl-out,
swirl-in effect whose colour shifts counter-clockwise. This replaces the
two-cell lemniscate walk that the frame in the tree draws, and it replaces the
figure-eight specification in the reader's earlier notes. The earlier figure
was two loops in opposite directions; this one is three segments across six
cells, and the two are not the same animation.

## Consequences

A prompt that lies about the hostname is harmless and a prompt that is honest
would be confusing, since the program is not a shell and the reader is not root.
The joke is load-bearing: it says what the field is and nothing about the
machine.

Input errors clearing on a timeout means the field has a state that is not its
contents. A reader who typed something wrong and looked away sees it recover,
which is the point, and a reader who wants it to stay has to read it faster.

Tool output off by default means a long run of file reads is invisible until
`/verbose`. That is what the reader asked for, and it costs a reader who wants
to watch the model work.

The twiddle is six cells rather than two, so it takes three columns where the
tree's takes two. Anything laid out against the twiddle has to account for
that, and the layout is open.

Colour shifting counter-clockwise is one less thing to derive. The tree
interpolates between six primaries over four seconds; a hue rotation with a
direction is shorter and is what is specified.

## What is open

- How long an input error stays before it clears. It is a timeout the reader
  will form a feel for, and the first value will be wrong.
- Which tools get an icon, and what the icon is when the terminal cannot draw
  one.

## Alternatives considered

Input errors as rows in the live view. Rejected in the decision: a row is the
record of what happened, and an input error never became a question.

A prompt computed from the environment. Rejected: it would have to be honest,
and the reader named a joke.

Tool output on by default. Rejected for the reason the reader gave, which is
that it is what makes a reply unreachable without scrolling.
