# 0000018 The bell rings for an error or a reply that needs an answer, in a pane that is not focused

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-05
- Supersedes: nothing. It settles question 6 in `QUESTIONS.md`

## Context

`QUESTIONS.md` question 6 asked what rings the pane bell, and named three
candidate events as different things: a reply arriving, a reply that needs an
answer, and an error. It warned that a bell ringing on every arrival is a bell
the reader stops hearing.

A reply arriving is the common case. Most turns settle cleanly and most of
those are not the reader's business until they choose to look.

## Decision

**The bell rings for two of the three events.** An error, and a reply that
needs an answer, such as a permission or approval prompt blocking progress. A
plain reply arriving, with nothing further required of the reader, does not
ring the bell.

**The bell rings only in a pane that is not focused.** The focused pane is the
one the reader is already looking at, and a bell there reports what the
reader can already see.

## Reasoning

Error and needs-an-answer are both cases where the reader must act before the
pane can go on. A plain reply is not, and it is also the most frequent event,
so it is the one that would desensitize the reader first if it rang the bell.
Excluding it is what keeps the other two meaning something.

The focused pane needs no bell because the reader's attention is already
there. A bell that rings under what the reader is reading tells them nothing
they have not already seen happen.

## Consequences

A reader who wants to know the instant any background pane finishes, even
cleanly, does not get that from the bell. The pane bar's status colour is
where that question is answered instead, as `SAVED.md` records: status
carries colour when the pane is active.

A reader with several panes waiting on long replies hears the bell only for
the ones that need them, rather than once per pane as each settles.

## Alternatives considered

Ring for every arrival, regardless of focus. Rejected for the reason the
question itself gave: a bell heard constantly is a bell ignored, and it would
bury the two events that matter under the one that usually does not.

Ring for the focused pane too. Rejected. The reader is already looking at it;
a bell there duplicates what the screen already shows rather than calling
attention to something off-screen.

## What this leaves open

What rings in the pane bar itself versus what rings the terminal bell
character, and whether the two are the same signal, is not asked by question
6 and is not answered here.

## Notes

Nothing in the tree has changed. There is no bell in the tree and no pane set
for it to ring in.
