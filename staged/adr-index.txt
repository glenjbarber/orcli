## Reading order

0000001 and 0000002 are the frame that is being replaced and go together. 0000010
supersedes both in what it describes, and both stand as records of a decision
that was taken and then replaced.

0000003 and 0000004 are the connection and the model. 0000004 refers back to 0000003
because a probe and a move are different acts. Both describe behaviour in the
tree and neither is affected by the redesign.

0000005 is the decision to build on the external library, accepted on 2026-10-05,
and is the only record of the first five that describes something not in the tree. Its line counts measure the
frame this redesign replaces rather than a plan.

0000006 stands. 0000007 stands for a session per pane, pane 0 as main, `Ctrl+B` as a
non-latching prefix, program-owned scrollback, and a session log that survives
a crash. 0000008 and 0000009 are superseded by 0000010, and both stand as records of a
decision that was taken and then replaced.

0000010 is the API. It answers the questions 0000007 and 0000008 left open, it gives
the screen in full, and where it is silent a question is still open and is
listed at its end.

0000011 fills the one gap 0000010 left in the keys a reader touches, and it is read
with 0000010 rather than after it.

0000012 to 0000016 are answers rather than proposals. Each one settles a question
that was open, each one names the cost it accepted rather than presenting the
decision as free, and each one is read beside the record it extends. 0000012 sits
under 0000006 and does not change it, since where the queue lives was never what
0000006 decided. 0000013 and 0000014 are about restore and 0000015 is about the history,
which 0000011 left open. 0000016 is about the screen, which 0000008 left open and 0000010
settled into seven regions, and the six-region screen it describes is the one
after the pane bar went.

0000017 is about the records rather than the interface. Read it once, then use the
vocabulary file beside it.

## What is superseded, and where it went

The frame in the tree is described by 0000001 and 0000002. 0000010 replaces it.

`staged/status-interrupt.txt` made escape an unqualified interrupt that sends
nothing. 0000006 supersedes it.

The nine-row footer that `staged/status-*.txt` records describe existed only in
uncommitted work in a worktree that was removed. It is not in the tree and
nothing supersedes it.

The prompt was `root@localhost $ ` in 0000009 and is `root@lolhost @ ` in 0000010.
The reader wrote both and marked the second as deliberate.

**`UI-redesign.md`, held in the `loreloom` repository, supersedes ten records
here**: 0000001, 0000002, 0000007, 0000008, 0000009, 0000010, 0000016, 0000021,
0000037, and 0000041. These are the records that describe the same ground it
redefines - the overall screen regions, the two status bars and their fields,
and the prompt - not the full forty-one. It settles a full-screen terminal with
two small status panes at the bottom, one blank separator line, then the
prompt above that, and leaves the two panes' relative order open pending
further instruction.

It gives the prompt as `root@lolhost $ `, with a space on each side of the
dollar sign. This is not the string 0000010 settled on (`root@lolhost @ `, an
at-sign, not a dollar sign, and marked deliberate there) - that disagreement is
recorded here rather than resolved, since `UI-redesign.md` does not address
0000010's choice or say it is wrong.

0000001 and 0000007 in the pane arrangement were superseded in two steps rather than
one. 0000008 replaced the single frame with three vertical parts, and 0000010 replaced
that with the API. A session per pane survives from 0000007. Visible panes do not,
and the pane bar went with them.

## What these records are not

They are not a specification. `DESIGN.md` describes the interface being built,
and these records say why it is that way rather than what it does. 0000010 and
0000011 together are the closest thing to a specification here, and they are still
records of decisions.

They are not exhaustive. Smaller decisions live in the comments on the code
that carries them.

They are not rewritten when the code changes. A record that is edited to agree
with the code is no longer a record of what was decided, and the thing worth
keeping is the rejected alternatives and the corrections. Five records here
carry a correction or a replacement rather than a deletion: 0000005 keeps the
belief its first draft held about the clipboard, 0000006 keeps the escape rule it
replaced, 0000008 keeps the tiling it rejected, 0000009 keeps the prompt 0000010
replaced, and 0000010 keeps the keys it did not mention.

They are not all written for the same reader. 0000001 to 0000006 were written after
their work merged and describe the tree. 0000007 onward describe an interface that
is being designed, and a reader arriving at one of those should read
`DESIGN.md` first, since these records answer why rather than what.

They are not the only place a supersession is written down. The header on the
record is, and this table duplicates it rather than being a second source of
truth.
