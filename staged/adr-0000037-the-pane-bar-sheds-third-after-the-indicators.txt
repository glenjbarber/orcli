# 0000037 The pane bar sheds third, after the indicators and before the bar fields

- Status: accepted, and SUPERCEDED by `UI-redesign.md`, which supersedes every prior UI architecture ADR
- Date: 2026-10-05
- Supersedes: nothing. It settles question 25 in `QUESTIONS.md`, filling the
  gap 0000016 left and 0000019 reopened. It does not rewrite 0000016

## Context

0000016 settled a shed ladder for six regions, after the pane bar had been
withdrawn, and named the pane bar's place in that ladder as recorded by its
absence rather than settled. 0000019 restored the pane bar and reopened exactly
that gap as question 25.

0000016's ladder, unchanged: the scrollback and mouse indicators go first, then
the fields of each bar from the right, then a bar drops whole at one field
left, then the live window, which keeps a floor of one row. The prompt is
never dropped.

## Decision

**The pane bar sheds third: after the two indicators, before either status
bar sheds a field.** The ladder, with the pane bar's place filled in:

1. The scrollback indicator and the mouse indicator.
2. The pane bar, whole.
3. The fields of each bar, from the right, as 0000016 already gives them.
4. A bar, whole, at one field left.
5. The live window, floor of one row.

The prompt is still never dropped, drawn last, as 0000016 already settled.

## Reasoning

The pane bar reports navigation state, which pane exists and which is
focused, rather than the reader's present activity, the same test 0000016 used
to shed the indicators first. That argues for shedding it early, alongside
them.

It does not go alongside them, though, because losing it costs more than
losing either indicator. `[scrollback]` and the mouse indicator explain a
mode; the pane bar is the only place a reader sees what panes exist at all,
and 0000020 already settled that it does not scroll or fold, so once it is
gone a reader also loses whatever its truncation was still showing. That is
worth one more step of the ladder than the indicators get, but it still goes
ahead of the status bars, which carry live information, Provider, Model,
Status, that a reader mid-task is more likely to need than a list of pane
numbers.

## Consequences

A reader at a narrow terminal loses the pane bar before either status bar
sheds a single field. Switching panes by number, or finding the focused one,
has to go by memory or by `/pane next` and `/pane previous` stepping blind
once the bar is gone, the same gap 0000020 and question 26 already named for
truncation.

## Alternatives considered

Shedding the pane bar alongside the indicators, as a third item in the same
step rather than its own step. Rejected: it is cheaper to lose than the
status bars but not as cheap as an indicator, since an indicator reports a
mode and the pane bar is the only place pane identity is shown at all.

Shedding the pane bar after the status bars begin losing fields. Rejected:
the status bars carry the reader's present activity, which 0000016 already
held back for the live window and the prompt on the same reasoning.

## What this leaves open

Question 26, how a reader reaches a pane the bar has already truncated off
the edge, which is unaffected by where the bar sits in this ladder and is
not reopened here.
