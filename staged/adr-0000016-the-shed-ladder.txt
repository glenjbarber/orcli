## Context

`QUESTIONS.md` question 5 asked which of seven regions yields first when the
terminal is short: live window, scrollback indicator, mouse indicator, status
bar 1, status bar 2, prompt, pane bar. The prompt was held to the end because
a session with nowhere to type is a session with no way to go on, and the live
window was to yield last because a reader reading history is using it.

The reader named Verbosity as the field that falls off bar 1 and left bar 2
undecided. Six further questions were put as yes or no, each with a
recommendation, and the reader answered them one at a time.

`DESIGN.md` and `staged/adr-0000010-interface-api.txt` both draw seven regions and
are behind the documentation work in progress. This record describes the six
the reader answered about and names the disagreement rather than drawing a
screen that does not exist.

## Decision

**The ladder, in order of what is given up first.**

1. The scrollback indicator and the mouse indicator.
2. The fields of each bar, from the right: Verbosity then Bell on bar 1, and
   Approval, Stealth, Autosave, Context, Hostname on bar 2.
3. A bar, whole, at one field left.
4. The live window, which keeps at least one row and goes no lower.

**The prompt is never dropped and never clipped.** It is drawn last so it is
the row that survives, and it is the only region with no threshold at all.

**A shed region returns when the terminal grows.** The layout pass runs on every
resize, so a region returning is the absence of a shed rather than a second
piece of work, and no second resize is needed to bring it back.

## Reasoning

**The indicators go first because they are the cheapest thing to lose.** They
report a mode rather than an activity, and both report nothing in the state
where the terminal is short. The reader held the prompt because the session has
nowhere to go on and held the live window because a reader reading history is
using it; both of those are the reader's present activity, and an indicator is
not.

**The bars shed from the right rather than emptying gradually.** Bar 1 sheds
Verbosity first, which the reader had already named. Carried to its end, bar 2
sheds Approval, Stealth, Autosave, Context, Hostname. A bar is never left as one
floating field, so whether a field is present does not depend on how the bar
happens to be sized.

**The live window keeps a row.** Zero rows cannot tell a program that is
thinking from a program that has stopped, and one row is the whole difference
between idle and dead. It yields last because a reader reading history is using
it, which is the reason the question gave.

**Returning is free.** The layout already recomputes on resize, so this is a
consequence of how the layout is written rather than a second mechanism.

## Consequences

A reader at a narrow terminal loses the two things that tell them the terminal
is the problem. A reader who has not noticed has a live window and a prompt and
no way to know why the screen is smaller than they left it.

The live window losing its last row is not reachable. The prompt cannot be
reached at all.

## What this leaves open

Nothing. Every region on the current screen has a place in the ladder.

The pane bar would have sat below the bars had it stayed, and its place in the
ladder is recorded by its absence rather than settled.

## Alternatives considered

Indicators last. Rejected. A reader told `[scrollback]` is losing a row while
the Provider and Model they came to look at keep theirs has been told the wrong
thing about what matters.

Bars emptying one field at a time with no floor. Rejected, because a field's
presence would then depend on the exact width, and a resize that gains one
column would bring back the field a reader had stopped expecting.

The live window shed last of everything including the prompt. Rejected. The
prompt was named as held by the reader and the window as yielding last of the
rest, and putting the window below the prompt inverts the second without being
asked to.
