# Record status vocabulary

Five words, used in this order of strength: `accepted`, `proposed`,
`UNCONFIRMED`, `SUPERCEDED`, `rejected`.

## accepted

The record was taken and is current. It is what the interface is built to.

## proposed

The record argues for something rather than settling it, and nothing is built
to it yet. 0000005 is the only one: a proposal about tview, superseded by nothing
and superseding nothing.

## UNCONFIRMED

The record settles what is ruled out without settling what is taken. It
differs from `proposed` in direction: a proposed record argues for a
candidate that nothing yet builds to, while an unconfirmed record narrows the
field by eliminating candidates and leaves what remains undecided. A reader
of an unconfirmed record learns what will not be built and does not learn
what will.

An unconfirmed record is expected to be followed by another, later, that
either settles the remaining question (becoming `accepted`) or narrows it
further (staying `UNCONFIRMED`, with a new record named as the reason it is
still that way). It is not a placeholder for forgetting to decide; it is
written when elimination itself is worth keeping even though the choice is
not made.

0000024 is the only one so far: `pbcopy` and OSC 52 on Terminal.app are ruled
out, and the clipboard path itself is not.

## SUPERCEDED

The record was taken and a later one replaced part or all of it. The file is
kept and is not edited. What it says is what was decided at the time, and the
thing worth keeping is the replaced alternative rather than a version that
agrees with the current code.

A superseded record is read to find what was replaced, not to find what to
build. A reader who is about to build from a superseded record has made a
mistake and the record itself is what would have prevented it, since it names
its replacement in the header.

## rejected

The record was considered and not taken. There are none. A decision not taken
belongs in `QUESTIONS.md` and then in `ANSWERS.md` when it is settled, and a
record is written for what was decided rather than for what was passed over.
`QUESTIONS.md` is the record of what is still open and `ANSWERS.md` is the
record of what was answered and what it cost.

## What a status line carries

```
- Status: accepted
- Status: accepted, and SUPERCEDED by 0000010
- Status: proposed
- Status: accepted, and SUPERCEDED by 0000006, in the part where it made escape an
  unqualified interrupt that sends nothing
```

The word is capitalised because it is a state and not a sentence, and it is
carried in the header rather than in the prose so that a reader scanning
headers sees which records are current without reading sixteen files.

A record that supersedes something names it in the same header, under
`Supersedes`, and the two lines are read together. A reader who has only the
superseding record learns what it replaced, and a reader who has only the
superseded record learns what replaced it.

## What is not a status change

A record that narrows, extends or corrects an earlier one without replacing it
is not a supersession. 0000011 fills a gap 0000010 left and 0000012 to 0000016 answer
questions rather than replacing anything. All of them are `accepted`, and each
names what it extends.

A correction inside a record is not a status change either. 0000005 keeps the belief
its first draft held about the clipboard, and 0000006 keeps the escape rule it
replaced. Both are `accepted`, and a record edited to agree with later code is
no longer a record of what was decided.
