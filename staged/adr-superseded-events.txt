# Architecture decision record

Written at the reader's instruction on 2026-10-05, after the merge of
`feature/bottom-console-input` into `main` as `f24e0b7`. It records what that merge
superseded and why, so a reader arriving at the tree after it does not have to
reconstruct the argument from the commit messages.

Four decisions, in the order they were taken. Each says what was replaced, what
replaced it, and what was rejected.

---

## ADR 001: The frame owns the whole screen

**Status:** accepted, in `2d7bdbe` and merged as `f24e0b7`.

### What was replaced

Six footer rows drawn downward at the bottom of the screen. The log was a region
above them, and the scroll region told the terminal where the log began.

### What replaced it

Twenty rows at the top of the screen. Every row carries a one-character status
field in the first column and a log row in the second. The prompt is the last
row. There is no scroll region at all.

### Why

The region was the bug. `SetScrollRegion` took a row count and hardcoded the
first row to one, so the six rows a reader was typing into were inside the region
and a long log went over them, while the rows below it could never be scrolled
into and read as the output being capped.

A frame with one owner has no seam to get wrong. Removing the region removes the
arithmetic rather than fixing it, and the two symptoms went together because they
had one cause.

### Rejected

**Fix the region to `7;24r`.** Two signatures, and it keeps the frame's rule that
the reader's own scrollback is the transcript. It was the recommendation in the
design record and it is a smaller change.

**The alternate screen.** A pinned region and a live scrollback cannot both be
had on the primary screen. Taking the alternate screen would make the bars
redrawable per paint at the cost of the transcript not being live scrollback
while the program runs.

### What it costs

The reader's own scrollback is not the transcript. It is whatever the terminal
kept from before the program started. A reader scrolling past the program's
first row finds their shell, not the conversation.

## ADR 002: A status field is one character

**Status:** accepted, in `2d7bdbe`.

### What was replaced

Two bars of named fields, dropped whole when the bar was too narrow, in an order
by how much a reader loses by losing each.

### What replaced it

Twenty named one-character fields, and `barWidth` is 1 with `joinWithin` joining
the whole list at every width. A field is a glyph rather than a word.

### Why

A field that cannot say what it means in one character says nothing, so every
glyph is a letter a reader can read rather than a figure a reader has to learn.
Two fields are not one character and are named as exceptions: the state is
carried as the whole word since it is what a reader watches second by second, and
the prompt row is the prompt and what the reader typed since a row cannot be a
glyph and a prompt at once.

### Rejected

**Twenty rows of readable bars.** The reader asked for twenty status bars and
then for one character each, and the two together leave a 20 by 20 block of single
characters with no prompt and no log, which is not an interface.

**Keeping the two bars and dropping fields.** The field order was chosen for
exactly that loss, and the frame made it unnecessary.

### What it costs

A count field is one digit. A log holding nineteen thousand rows reports `1`. The
full number is in the transcript and the field names say which they are.

## ADR 003: There is no `/connect`

**Status:** accepted, in `140ff39`.

### What was replaced

`/connect` in the command table, with no handler. A listed command with no body is
one a reader types and is refused.

### What replaced it

Nothing. The entry is gone. A reader's own first question is the probe: a turn
that comes back with text has proved the credential and the model together, and
that is what writes the model to the configuration file. A turn that delivered
nothing confirms nothing, even when the endpoint called it finished.

### Why

The reader named it. There is no separate check, so there is nothing to keep in
step with one, and a model is written only on evidence rather than on a guess the
reader would find out about from a 400 on their next turn.

### Rejected

**`/connect` calling the models endpoint.** The transport has no such method, so
that is a new method and a new parse to keep working. A key that authenticates
but is not entitled to the model list would be unable to write anything at all.

**A dedicated probe request.** One request either way, and a fixed probe is a row
the reader did not type sitting above the answer to what they did.

## ADR 004: A choice records the model it replaced

**Status:** accepted, in `b1950ab`.

### What was replaced

`WriteModel`, which set the `model` member and nothing else.

### What replaced it

`WriteModelSwap`, which sets `model` and records the one it replaced under
`last_model`. `/model NAME` chooses and records; `/model last` swaps the pair.
Two `last` commands put the members back where they were, which is a test and not
a claim.

### Why

A reader who typed a model and then typed `/model last` is asking to come back to
where they were, and that only works if the model they left was written down when
they left it. The swap is its own inverse, so pressing it twice is safe.

The two members are written in one pass. Two reads and two writes would leave a
reader whose terminal died between them holding a file that disagrees with itself.

A choice moves the session as well as the file, through a new `Session.SetModel`,
so the frame does not draw one model while the reader is answered by another.

### Rejected

**`/connect` remembering the model.** There is no `/connect`.

**A confirmation recording the model it replaced.** A reader's first question is
a probe, not a move. Treating it as one would send `/model last` to a model they
never chose.

## What superseded the reference documentation

`doc/` held five files written from a build this branch is not: `README.md`,
`interface-connect.md`, `interface-bell.md`, `interface-verbosity.md`,
`interface-permission.md`, and `interface-commands.md`. None of the files they
name exists in this tree, and they describe a header-and-pane frame, a
`LineEditor` with panes and a hint row, a thirty-two row table, and seven
verbosity levels where this branch has six.

The branch supersedes them. Two of them were overwritten by the merge and are
backed up in `doc-backup/`, untracked, byte for byte as they were:

```text
doc-backup/README.md
doc-backup/interface-commands.md
```

The other four are untouched and still untracked. They are not reconciled, since
the branch supersedes what they describe and a reader wanting the older build's
account has them.

The record is in `staged/design-doc-superseded.txt`, and the five files describing
this build are in `doc/` as of the merge.

## What is still open

Four things a reader changing this code will meet, listed so absence reads as a
gap rather than as oversight.

1. **A turn carries no history.** `ask` sends one user message and no
   conversation, so a follow-up question is blind to what came before it.

2. **`internal/verbosity` is untracked and unimported.** The six-level ladder is
   the specification per the supersession record, and nothing reaches the wire. The
   frame carries a letter `v` in `fieldVerbosity` and no level is asked for.

3. **`DrawStack` and `DrawFooterRow` are exported over an unexported type.** A
   caller outside the module can call them and cannot construct the argument.

4. **The frame is unverified on a real terminal.** Every test draws to a buffer.
   Whether twenty rows and a caret on row twenty look right needs a screen, and the
   design record says so.
