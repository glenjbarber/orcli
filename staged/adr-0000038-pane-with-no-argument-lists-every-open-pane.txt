# 0000038 /pane with no argument lists every open pane

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-05
- Supersedes: nothing. It settles question 26 in `QUESTIONS.md`

## Context

0000020 settled that the pane bar neither scrolls nor folds, and truncates
silently when there are more panes than it has columns for, with nothing
marking that more exist. That left open how a reader reaches a pane the bar
is not showing.

`/pane N` switches to pane N and `/pane next` / `/pane previous` step
through panes, wrapping at both ends, already per `SAVED.md`. Neither of
those needs the bar to show every pane: stepping is blind by nature, and
switching by number only needs the reader to already know the number.

## Decision

**`/pane`, with no argument, lists every open pane number**, printed as a
line in the live window. It does not touch the bar, and the bar's
truncation from 0000020 is unaffected.

## Reasoning

The bar's job is a glanceable, bounded-width status line; the question
"what panes exist" is an on-demand, unbounded answer. 0000020's truncation
problem came from one region trying to do both at once. Giving `/pane` this
job instead keeps the two separate rather than growing the bar to cover a
case it was never sized for.

`/pane next` and `/pane previous` already do not depend on seeing the bar,
so the only real gap was a reader who has lost track entirely and wants the
full set back. `/pane` with no argument is exactly that answer and nothing
more.

## Consequences

A reader who cannot see a pane in the truncated bar now has one more
keystroke to fall back on rather than none. This does not make the bar's
truncation less lossy; it gives the reader an escape from it elsewhere.

## Alternatives considered

Making the bar itself scroll or fold to show every pane. Rejected already
by 0000020 directly, and not reopened here.

A dedicated command distinct from `/pane`, such as `/panes`. Not raised;
`/pane` with no argument reads naturally as "tell me about the panes" and
needs no second command name.
