# Decision: a long paste is folded for display and expanded by a command

Chosen at the reader's request, over writing the paste into the log as rows above
the prompt and over refusing a paste too long to show.

## The choice

A paste containing one or more newlines is **one prompt**, with the newlines
embedded in it rather than each line becoming its own submission. A paste too long
to show is folded for display and **the full content is sent unchanged**.

The prompt row carries the folded head plus a count of what is held, and a command
expands the remainder into the log, where the reader can read it and select it. The
prompt row stays quiet and the log is left for the conversation rather than for
what was typed.

## Why a count and a command over the log

The log is the transcript of a session, and a paste is something the reader typed
rather than something that was said to them. Filling it with text the reader just
pasted puts a reader who scrolls back through a session reading their own input
again, and it is the one thing in the log that is not part of the conversation.

Writing it into the log as rows above the prompt would have needed nothing new,
which was the reason to prefer it. It is not the right one, since the same reader
scrolling back is the one who would read those rows, and the prompt row is where a
reader looks when they want to see what they are about to send.

The command is what makes the remainder reviewable, since the head alone would
show a reader who pasted something long and unexpected a head and a count with
nothing between them. The command shows the remainder in the log at the reader's
request, and nothing about the prompt row changes when it runs.

A refusal would have been the third shape, and it is wrong for the reason
`AGENTS.md` holds: a call always produces a result. A paste the reader asked for
refused because it is long would be a refusal of work they asked for, on a ground
that has nothing to do with whether the paste is safe.

## What this settles for the input unit

- Bracketed paste is turned on when the interface starts and off when it exits,
  since there is no screen any more to bracket it. The mode belongs to the
  terminal, so the exit is what the ordering protects.
- A paste with newlines is one prompt, not several submissions.
- A paste too long for the prompt row is folded, and the head plus a count is what
  the prompt row carries.

## Folded, not cut

A line longer than the terminal is **folded at a column**, not cut from the tail.
The reader sees the whole line at their terminal width and selects it without wrap
columns coming along.

This is settled now rather than left to the unit that adds the command, since it
is a change to how every row reaches the log rather than a decision about one
command. A pasted line of code is what a reader pastes, and it is what they need
to select and copy whole; a row cut from the tail loses the end of a line that was
never too long to be shown, and a row hard-wrapped at the width leaves the wrap
columns in the selection.

The measurement already exists in `internal/tui/width.go`, which counts display
columns and handles East Asian wide characters and combining marks, so folding
uses it rather than counting bytes.

## What this does not change

`CutColumnFromEnd` stays. It is what keeps the log and the screen agreeing about
what was said, and a model reply that runs past the terminal is still cut rather
than folded, since a fold would rewrite a reply into several rows and a reader
reading the log would see a sentence that was never written that way.

The fold applies to what the reader is about to send: a pasted line, and the
remainder the expanding command writes. It does not apply to the transcript.

## The command

The command that expands the remainder is its own unit, since a reader who pasted
something long needs it only when they pasted something long, and building it with
the input unit makes a unit whose subject is input carry a command the interface
cannot use without input. Its name is not settled.

## Unverified

Nothing here was run against a terminal. Whether the folded head and count read
correctly on a real prompt row is not settled by a test, and whether a folded
remainder selects cleanly is not either.
